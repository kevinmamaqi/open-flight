# Williams tandem glider

Open Flight’s `tandem-1` is a cut-and-fold flight trial based on Archibald Williams’s Model B. Williams describes two pairs of wings arranged one behind the other, a folded paper head and an optional sliding metal clip for balance. Figure 128 shows both Model B and a separate three-pair Model C. The Open Flight sheet uses Model B. ## Source and print geometry

The facsimile identifies its edition as Thomas Nelson and Sons, 1918. Project Gutenberg records Williams’s dates as 1871–1934 and marks the book public domain in the United States. His death is also beyond the 80-year term Spain retains for authors deceased before 7 December 1987. Figure 128 carries no separate artist credit. The Open Flight SVG is a new technical drawing using the historical planform and a new set of dimensions. [Facsimile edition](https://chestofbooks.com/crafts/popular-mechanics/Things-To-Make/) · [Project Gutenberg catalogue](https://dev.gutenberg.org/ebooks/14664) · [Spanish Intellectual Property Act, Fourth Transitional Provision](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-8930#dtcuaa)

The SVG viewport is 162 × 90 mm. At 100% scale the cut outline is 152 mm long and 80 mm across the front pair. The rear pair spans 72 mm and the center keel band is 8 mm wide on the flat blank. The paper nose rolls along four creases spaced 7 mm apart. The line patterns identify the cut, keel, wing roots and nose folds on a grayscale print. The viewport leaves 5 mm between the untrimmed template edge and the nearest outer cut coordinate.

Williams gives a rough profile and fold directions. This redraw fixes the span, wing sweep, keel width and fold positions for repeatable printing. Its fourfold paper nose is the baseline. An adult can add a small single clip near the nose in a later trim trial after recording the paper-only result, following Williams’s suggestion of adjustable ballast.

## First flight trial

This revision has no physical build or flight record. Print at 100% and verify the blank length and front wingspan before cutting. Start on ordinary printer paper and record its weight, the nose folds and the wingtip heights. Measure five gentle, level launches at each setting. Start with level wings, then compare a second setting with all four tips lifted by similar angles. Record first floor contact and sideways drift, including mislaunches. Record any ballast change as a separate setting. Williams’s account of a stable glide describes his own model. Open Flight still needs to test this geometry.

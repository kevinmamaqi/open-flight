# Open Flight brand core

## Who it is for

Children aged 5 to 12 who make the thing, and the grown-up next to them: a parent at the kitchen table or a teacher with thirty copies to print. The child holds the scissors and the paper. The adult reads the page, sets up the printer and asks the next question. Every screen is read first by the adult, often on a phone, and every sheet is read by both of them.

## What it promises

**One sheet of paper, a real flying thing, and a question you can answer yourselves.** It is free, open and honest about what has and has not been tested. The design that prints is the design on the screen, at the right size, with folds you can follow.

## Personality

| Trait | In practice |
| --- | --- |
| **Hands-on** | The paper is the product. Pages lead to a printout, a fold or a launch. Words are verbs: cut, fold, clip, launch, measure. We show a picture of the fold before describing it. |
| **Curious, not cute** | We treat a child as a young scientist: predict, try, change one thing, compare. No mascots, no exclamation marks, no baby talk. A 12-year-old and a teacher should both feel respected. |
| **Precise and honest** | We give real sizes in mm, say "untested" when that is the case, and credit every source. We never promise a result the paper has not shown. |
| **Calm** | Paper-white pages, one strong colour for the next action and plenty of space. The excitement comes from the flight, not from the interface. |

## Voice

Short sentences. One instruction per sentence. Name the object, then what to do with it. Use "you" for the builder and "a grown-up" for the adult. Russian uses the polite plural *вы* and plain imperatives. Spanish uses *tú*.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Print at 100%. Measure the 50 mm line before you cut. | Make sure your printer settings are correct before proceeding! |
| Печатайте в масштабе 100%. Перед вырезанием измерьте линию 50 мм. | Обязательно проверьте настройки принтера, прежде чем продолжить! |
| Change one thing, then launch from the same spot. | Experiment with endless possibilities and become a flight master! |
| Измените что-то одно и запустите с того же места. | Экспериментируйте без границ и станьте настоящим асом полётов! |
| This fold is untested. Tell us how it flew. | Our revolutionary design flies further than ever. |
| Этот сгиб ещё не испытан. Расскажите, как он полетел. | Наша революционная модель летает дальше всех. |
| A grown-up can make the small cuts. | Ask mommy or daddy for help, little pilot! |
| Маленькие надрезы может сделать взрослый. | Попроси маму или папу помочь, маленький пилот! |

## Visual directions considered

**A. Field notebook.** The workshop as a naturalist's and test pilot's notebook: warm uncoated paper, graphite ink, a narrow book serif for names and a very legible text face for instructions. Specimen labels for butterflies and aircraft, a measured grid behind drawings, and the flight log as a first-class object. Colour is used like ink on a page: one deep air-blue for information and one vermilion for the next action. The notation of folding (dashed valley, dash-dot mountain, solid cut) is the only decoration. It needs almost no ink and reads the same on a phone and on a cheap mono laser printer.

**B. Fold diagram.** A strict translation of origami notation into an interface: pure line drawing, a geometric sans and monospaced measurements, primary colours for each fold type, everything set on an engineering grid with dimension arrows. It is the most "technical" and the most consistent with the sheet. It is also cold for a five-year-old, and it relies on colour-coded line types that fail in grayscale unless every line is also drawn differently.

**C. Air mail.** The romance of flight post: air-mail chevron borders in red and blue, postage-stamp frames for each design, luggage-tag labels, cancellation-stamp badges, and a display face with period character. It is warm and memorable. On paper it costs ink, stripes compete with the child's own colouring, and its nostalgia speaks to grown-ups more than to the child.

**Chosen: A, Field notebook.** It is the only direction that is native to both halves of the product. The notebook is literally what a family makes: they predict, launch, record and compare. Its look is mostly paper and line, so it obeys the print rules by default (low ink, grayscale-safe, meaning carried by line style). It gives the adult an object with integrity and gives the child the role of investigator rather than audience. From B it borrows the fold notation as the system's single graphic language. From C it borrows nothing except the vermilion of a paper dart.

Everything below derives from that choice: the symbol is a sheet with a valley fold and a launch path; colours are paper, graphite, air-blue and kite-vermilion; type is a book serif (Alegreya) for names and a literacy face (Andika, shipped as Open Flight Text) for everything a person must read and do.

How it flies

Paper clips on the nose
Back edges

Needle hardly glides at all. Your arm gives it all its speed, and it keeps that speed for as long as it can, like a thrown javelin.

Almost the whole sheet is wrapped round one narrow triangle. That makes a hard, thin body with tiny wings. There is very little to rub against the air, so it slows down slowly.

The wraps stack paper at the front. Folded on A4, Needle should balance about 98 mm behind the nose, well ahead of its small wings. A dart balanced that far forward points itself along its path, like an arrow.

It needs a long room or a calm day outside, and a firm, level throw. Never throw it towards anyone: the nose is sharp. Our version is untested. Tell us how far it went.

Make it

What you need One rectangular sheet and a ruler. You can start with plain paper.

  1. Step 1 / 11

    Fold the long diagonal

    Cut out the sheet along the thick border. Print side up, short edge at the top. Fold the top edge down onto the right edge. The crease runs from the top right corner. Press it flat.

  2. Step 2 / 11

    Fold to a pencil line

    Mark the folded edge 138.1 mm from its lower end and the bottom edge 42.5 mm from the left corner. Join the marks with a pencil line and fold the lower left part over on it. The first mark becomes the nose.

  3. Step 3 / 11

    Fold the edge onto the layers

    Pivot on the nose mark. Fold the new folded edge over until it lies on the raw edge underneath. Wrap the layers as tightly as you can.

  4. Step 4 / 11

    Mark the core

    Measure 188.6 mm from the nose along both long edges and join the marks. Crease the line both ways, then open it. This narrow triangle is the core of the plane.

  5. Step 5 / 11

    Tuck the back

    Fold everything that sticks out past the back line inside, over the line.

  6. Step 6 / 11

    First wrap

    Fold everything outside the core over its right long side, all layers together. Tuck anything that crosses the back line inside again.

  7. Step 7 / 11

    Second wrap

    Now fold everything outside over the left long side, turning the same way as before. Tuck the back again.

  8. Step 8 / 11

    Keep wrapping

    Keep wrapping the remaining paper round the core, side after side, as tightly as you can. Stop when the small free triangle near the nose is covered. One loose flap is left over.

  9. Step 9 / 11

    Fold the core in half

    Turn the core nose up. Fold it in half along its middle, the right half behind the left. The loose flap goes with it.

  10. Step 10 / 11

    Fold one wing, then match its twin

    Fold the upper wing down on a line from the nose to a point 12 mm from the centre fold at the tail. Near the nose, roll the thick layers as far as they go. Turn over and fold the other wing to match.

  11. Step 11 / 11

    Lock the body

    Wrap the loose flap round the body, over the centre fold and back over the wing root, and press it flat. Nothing should flap loose: the body is hard and smooth. Open the wings level, a thin T from behind.

Launch it

Hold it near the back, a finger on the back edge. Throw hard and level, like a javelin, never towards anyone.

Trim it

If it…Try this
Curves awayMake the two thin wings match. Press any loose wrap flat.
Dives into the floorBend up the back 15 mm of each wing, next to the body, until the back corner sits 1 mm above the wing. Still diving: bend 1 mm more.
Rolls over and spirals downLift each tip to about 10 mm above the body fold, both at the same height.

What to try. These fixes follow how paper gliders behave. They have not yet been tested on this design.

Why it exists

Where it comes from

Lazar Krstić from Serbia won the distance final of Red Bull Paper Wings at Hangar-7 in Salzburg on 14 May 2022 with a throw of 61.11 m. Guinness lists it as the farthest throw at that championship; the overall distance record belongs to other designs. Foldable Flight teaches his plane as the “Red Dart”.

Needle is Open Flight’s version of it. The fold sequence is derived from Krstić’s design; the diagrams, text and art are our own, drawn from the fold geometry. Nothing is copied from the videos. Red Bull and Guinness appear only as facts; there is no endorsement.

It borrows the whole fold of the Red Dart: every crease after the first pivots on one point, which becomes the nose.

Try this

Do tight wraps fly straighter?

Predict first. Predict first: which copy lands closer to a straight line, the loose one or the tight one?

  1. Fold two Needles from the same paper: wrap one loosely and one as tightly as you can.
  2. Mark a straight line on the floor from where you stand.
  3. Throw each copy five times along the line with the same hard, level push.
  4. Mark where each one first touches the floor.
  5. Measure how far each mark is from the line.

What to look for. Look for wide curves from the loose copy: a layer that lifts in flight works like a small rudder.

Go deeper

Needle is 187 mm long and only 23 mm across the wings, an aspect ratio of 0.24. Its 21 cm² of wing carry 4.4 g of paper, about 2.1 kg per square metre: seven times the load of the gliders in this chapter. At a lift coefficient of 0.6 that means 7–8 m/s just to stay up, so it flies on the speed of the throw, not on its wings. The balance point (98 mm) sits about 20 % of the wing’s average depth ahead of the estimated centre of lift (122–125 mm): very stable, which is what a distance dart wants. These figures are estimates from the fold geometry.

A distance throw is mostly a ballistic flight: the arm gives the energy in a fraction of a second and drag spends it. A glider with big wings would climb, slow down and float; a dart keeps its speed. Contest rules for distance ask for one standard A4 sheet, folded without cuts, so practise with the whole-sheet build.

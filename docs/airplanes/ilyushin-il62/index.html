Fold guide

What you need One rectangular sheet and a ruler. You can start with plain paper.

  1. Step 1 / 7

    Find the centre

    Cut out the sheet along the thick border. Print side up. Put the short edge at the top. Bring the two long edges together, press the middle crease, then open the sheet again.

  2. Step 2 / 7

    Weight the front edge

    Fold the top edge down to the 36 mm mark. This makes an 18 mm band across the sheet.

  3. Step 3 / 7

    Fold the hemmed corners

    Using the new front edge, fold both corners to the centre. Smooth each thick diagonal outwards from the tip.

  4. Step 4 / 7

    Shape the swept shoulders

    Fold each sloping shoulder inward on line 4. The crease runs from the nose to a mark 160 mm down each side from the new front. The folded edges stay apart.

  5. Step 5 / 7

    Close the body

    Keep the folded details face up. Fold the right half away from you, behind the left, along the centre line. The folded details will be on the outside.

  6. Step 6 / 7

    Fold one wing, then match its twin

    Hold the closed body with the centre edge on your right. Fold only the upper wing outward on the line 22 mm from that edge. Turn the model over and fold the other wing to match. From behind, the wings make a T with the body hanging below.

  7. Step 7 / 7

    Raise the wing tips

    Open the wings to a shallow V. Turn up the outer 10 mm of each long side until both tips stand upright. Sight from the tail and match the heights.

Try this flight test

Make one copy with flat tips and one with 10 mm tips. Give each five level launches. Record any left or right landings before changing another fold.

Swept shoulders · striped tail. Turning both tips up changes the area facing sideways. If one tip is bent more, the two sides can feel different forces. Compare equal tips first, then change one fold and track the first sideways motion.

Aircraft origins

Moscow, USSR (now Russia)

Ilyushin’s team in Moscow built a Soviet jet for long passenger routes. The prototype first flew in January 1963.
